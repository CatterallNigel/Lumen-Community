# ALL IN 2026 --- Aestimare Research Leads

**Status:** Research watchlist\
**Research area:** Model behaviour / Aestimare\
**Source event:** ALL IN 2026, Montréal, 16--17 September 2026\
**Purpose:** Identify people, research, talks, and adjacent work that
may contribute useful evidence, methodology, concepts, or counterpoints
to the development of Lumen Aestimare and its Periti Assessores.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose

ALL IN 2026 brings together more than 260 speakers across AI research,
model development, deployment, governance, infrastructure, and society.
The purpose of this note is not to catalogue the conference or endorse
the positions of its speakers.

It is a **research-leads document**.

The filter is deliberately narrow:

> Does this person's work contribute something potentially useful to
> understanding, observing, reproducing, evaluating, or characterising
> model behaviour?

Particular interest includes:

-   observable model behavioural characteristics;
-   repeated and controlled evaluation;
-   reproducibility and experimental methodology;
-   behavioural change over time or context;
-   ambiguity, contradiction, uncertainty, and model response;
-   trust and assurance grounded in evidence;
-   operational evaluation and responsible deployment;
-   the relationship between model capability and observed behaviour;
-   evidence preservation, provenance, and longitudinal assessment.

This aligns with Aestimare's working research position:

> A single execution is an observation. Repeated controlled executions
> become an experiment. Multiple experiments begin to characterise
> behaviour. Longitudinal evidence allows us to detect behavioural
> change.

The people below are therefore research leads rather than authorities
whose positions Lumen necessarily adopts.

## 2. Initial Speaker Shortlist

### 2.1 Yoshua Bengio --- Very High Priority

**Current relevance**

Bengio's recent work on advanced AI safety examines problematic or
unintended model behaviours including agency, deception,
self-preservation, and behaviour that diverges from designer or user
intent.

For Aestimare, the important connection is not simply the broad question
of whether a model is "safe." It is the narrower empirical question:

> What behavioural characteristics can be observed, reproduced,
> characterised, and compared?

This makes Bengio's work potentially useful for identifying **classes of
behaviour worth observing** and for considering what evidence is
required before a behavioural concern can be treated as more than an
isolated occurrence.

**Research questions for Aestimare**

-   Which behaviours are treated as observable rather than inferred?
-   How are undesirable behaviours elicited or detected?
-   What distinguishes an anecdotal observation from reproducible
    evidence?
-   How much does behaviour depend upon context, framing, tools, or
    interaction history?
-   Can any of these behavioural classes become specialised Periti?
-   How are uncertainty and false positives handled?

**Existing material to review**

-   *The Catastrophic Risks of AI --- and a Safer Path* --- TED talk,
    2025.
-   Recent LawZero publications and presentations.
-   Relevant work concerning model agency, deception, self-preservation,
    and safety evaluation.

**LinkedIn / network action**

Follow first. Direct contact is not presently necessary; the immediate
value is in following the research and identifying concepts that
intersect with Aestimare.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.2 Joelle Pineau --- Very High Priority

**Current relevance**

Pineau is particularly relevant through work on empirical methodology,
reproducibility, evaluation, and responsible AI.

This intersects directly with Aestimare's need to distinguish:

**observation → repetition → experiment → characterisation →
longitudinal evidence**

Aestimare cannot make useful behavioural claims merely because an
interesting response occurred once. The methodology used to establish
repeatability, control variables, preserve experimental conditions, and
report uncertainty is therefore foundational.

**Research questions for Aestimare**

-   What constitutes adequate reproducibility in model evaluation?
-   Which experimental variables need to be recorded and controlled?
-   How should repeated observations be reported?
-   How should non-deterministic results be treated?
-   What standards of evidence justify a behavioural characterisation?
-   How should evaluation results expose limitations and uncertainty?

**Existing material to review**

Prioritise Pineau's work and talks on:

-   reproducibility;
-   empirical methodology;
-   evaluation;
-   responsible AI;
-   reporting standards and experimental evidence.

**LinkedIn / network action**

High-value person to follow. Review current public work before
considering any interaction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.3 Tulsee Doshi --- High Priority

**Current relevance**

Doshi's work is useful because it sits near the boundary where
responsible-AI principles, model evaluation, product decisions, and
operational deployment meet.

That is important for Aestimare because behavioural evidence ultimately
needs to be useful outside the research experiment itself.

A useful conceptual chain is:

**principle → observable characteristic → evaluation → evidence →
operational decision**

Aestimare should not attempt to declare an abstract model "trustworthy."
It should provide evidence from which a person or system can make a
calibrated, purpose-dependent trust decision.

**Research questions for Aestimare**

-   How are broad responsible-AI concerns converted into measurable
    evaluations?
-   Which evaluations are useful operationally?
-   How are evaluation results communicated to product or governance
    decisions?
-   Where is the boundary between measurement and judgement?
-   How are limitations, uncertainty, and context represented?

**Existing material to review**

Prioritise public talks, papers, interviews, and presentations
concerning:

-   responsible AI;
-   model evaluation;
-   safety evaluation;
-   deployment decisions;
-   evaluation methodology.

**LinkedIn / network action**

Worth following and potentially engaging with if a specific point of
intersection emerges.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.4 Aidan Gomez --- Medium--High Priority

**Current relevance**

Gomez provides a useful model-builder and enterprise-deployment
perspective.

Aestimare research should not be constructed solely from safety,
governance, or evaluation perspectives. It also needs to understand how
model providers describe capabilities, limitations, reliability,
enterprise requirements, and changes in model behaviour.

This provides an important counterweight:

> What do the people building and deploying foundation models believe
> needs to be measured, guaranteed, or understood?

**Research questions for Aestimare**

-   How are capability and reliability distinguished?
-   What behavioural guarantees do enterprise users expect?
-   Which model changes materially affect downstream behaviour?
-   How do providers discuss consistency, reasoning, tool use, and
    uncertainty?
-   What evidence do enterprise deployments require beyond benchmark
    performance?

**Existing material to review**

Review selectively rather than comprehensively, prioritising material
on:

-   model reliability;
-   enterprise deployment;
-   reasoning;
-   evaluation;
-   trust;
-   model capability changes.

**LinkedIn / network action**

Follow selectively. Of particular interest when discussion moves beyond
general model capability into reliability, evaluation, or enterprise
assurance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.5 Arthur Mensch --- Medium Priority / Watch

**Current relevance**

Mensch offers another frontier-model-provider perspective, particularly
around model architecture, efficiency, reasoning capability, deployment,
and open models.

The relevance to Aestimare is presently indirect. His work becomes more
interesting where it touches behavioural consistency, model limitations,
evaluation, or the effects of model architecture and deployment choices
on observable behaviour.

**Research questions for Aestimare**

-   What behavioural consequences follow from model or deployment
    architecture?
-   How are reasoning capability and reliability described?
-   What limitations are acknowledged by the provider?
-   Does open-model deployment alter the assurance problem?
-   Which provider-side metrics correlate poorly or well with observable
    behaviour?

**Existing material to review**

Watch for talks or interviews specifically concerning:

-   reasoning models;
-   evaluation;
-   consistency;
-   limitations;
-   open-model deployment;
-   enterprise reliability.

**LinkedIn / network action**

Watch rather than investigate deeply unless a stronger Aestimare
intersection emerges.

## 4. Deliberate Exclusions

ALL IN uses terms such as *behavioural analysis* in several contexts.

Those references should not automatically be treated as relevant to
Aestimare.

For example, behavioural analysis used for customer segmentation,
personalisation, advertising, or retail analytics primarily concerns
**human/customer behaviour**. Aestimare's present research focus is
**observable model behaviour**.

There may eventually be useful intersections with human behavioural
psychology and Emotional AI, particularly where human state or
interaction affects subsequent model behaviour, but the domains should
not be conflated simply because they share the word *behavioural*.

## 5. Adjacent Research Leads

During the initial ALL IN research, two recent papers surfaced whose
terminology and subject matter appear sufficiently close to Lumen's
Reasoning Assurance direction to justify separate review.

### 5.1 Assurance Closure

**Paper:** arXiv:2608.07317

Initial search results describe an assurance process concerned with
establishing what must be true, obtaining evidence, judging evidence
credibility, preserving validity through change, and using uncertainty
to constrain agent authority.

**Why this matters**

The terminology and conceptual structure appear adjacent to Lumen's
Reasoning Assurance position:

-   assurance based upon evidence;
-   validity through change;
-   uncertainty as an explicit property;
-   bounded authority based upon available assurance.

**Action**

Read the complete paper before drawing architectural conclusions.
Specifically determine:

-   what the authors mean by *assurance*;
-   what constitutes evidence;
-   whether evidence is behavioural, formal, operational, or mixed;
-   how change invalidates or preserves prior assurance;
-   whether their concept overlaps with or differs materially from
    Lumen's accumulated-evidence model.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5.2 Long-Term Evidence and Reasoning Histories

**Paper:** arXiv:2608.19511

Initial search results describe maintaining durable histories of agent
activity, evidence, assumptions, and reasoning so that later trust
assessments can be made for a particular purpose.

**Why this matters**

This appears directly adjacent to several Lumen concerns:

-   provenance;
-   accumulated evidence;
-   preserved assumptions;
-   reasoning history;
-   longitudinal assessment;
-   purpose-dependent trust.

It may provide useful external comparison for Lumen's distinction
between preserving reasoning provenance and merely preserving generated
prose.

**Action**

Review independently and compare its architecture and terminology
against:

-   Vestigare;
-   Repetere;
-   Lumen continuity/provenance;
-   Aestimare;
-   Reasoning Assurance.

Do not assume conceptual equivalence from terminology alone.

## 10. Research Principle

This document is intentionally a watchlist rather than a literature
review.

Names, papers, talks, and concepts belong here because they may help
answer a question relevant to Aestimare. Inclusion does not imply
agreement, endorsement, or adoption.

The useful outcome is not a larger bibliography.

It is a better answer to:

> **What can Lumen observe and preserve that allows Aestimare to make
> defensible, reproducible statements about model behaviour and
> behavioural change?**
